Wiring Reference — Two Independent Halves

Every pin below is named the way it is printed on your board: raw nRF52840 port labels (P0.22, P1.15) rather than Pro Micro D-numbers. The smaller grey line under each assignment carries the Pro Micro alias, because most ZMK shields and tutorials still speak that dialect — they are two names for one physical pin. Each half is its own nice!nano with its own battery, switch and reset button; there is no wire between the halves, they pair over BLE.

Which side am I looking at?

The nice!nano prints its pin labels on the underside. Every pinout on this page is drawn from that side — the same face you photographed — so what you read here matches what you read on the board while you solder. Flip the board over and the two columns trade places.

Right half — nice!nano #2

Bottom view silkscreen side · USB-C at top · RAW column on the left

The nice!view has no D/C pin. It is a Sharp memory-in-pixel LCD (sharp,ls0xx), which takes chip-select, clock and data only — an earlier version of this page showed a D/C line on P0.06 that does not exist on the part. Display signals now sit on P0.10, P1.01 and P1.11, matching the firmware's spi3. Two of them land on underside pads rather than header pins, because the header has exactly one free pin left.

Two things a pin table can't show

Before you pick up the iron

  • P0.09 and P0.10 are the NFC pins. On the nRF52840 those two default to NFC antenna duty and only behave as ordinary GPIO once the firmware sets CONFIG_NFCT_PINS_AS_GPIO=y. ZMK's nice_nano_v2 board definition enables it, so a stock ZMK build is fine — but if you ever move to a custom board definition, confirm it is still set or COL 1 and the display MOSI line go dead.
  • Peripheral pins are a choice, not a constraint. The nRF52840 muxes any GPIO to any serial peripheral through pinctrl, so I²C on P0.17/P0.20 and SPI on P1.13/P0.10 are conventions picked to keep the matrix contiguous — not fixed-function pins. Move them if your layout wants it, and update pinctrl to match.
  • Both dialects appear in ZMK configs. &gpio0 22 and &pro_micro 4 address the same physical pin. Pick one and stay with it across your matrix transform, encoder and display nodes — mixing them in one file is where wiring bugs hide.
  • Two display signals are on the underside. nice!view MOSI goes to the P1.01 pad and the EC12 switch to P1.02, because the 18-pin header is fully committed once the matrix, trackball and encoder are wired. Those pads are smaller than the header holes — tin them first and use thin wire.
  • TPS65 RDY is optional. Some Azoteq TPS65 breakouts don't expose a data-ready line — if yours doesn't, leave P1.11 unused on the left half and poll the trackpad over I²C instead.
  • EC12 leg order varies by part. Confirm which of the five legs is the shared common (C) with a continuity beep before soldering — on most EC12s it's the centre pin of each 3-pin group, but check yours.
  • Battery polarity is not user-serviceable. Double-check +/− against the B+/B− silkscreen before connecting — reversed polarity can damage the charge circuit.
  • Diode direction must be consistent across all 60 keys (30 per half) and must match the diode-direction setting in your ZMK matrix transform, or every key will appear to do nothing.
